What every column on this report means

Three systems feed this page, and most confusion comes from mistaking one for another. The catalogue comes from the CMS database. The engagement numbers come from the analytics warehouse. Everything that is a rate, a share or a day-count is worked out from those two — and can be blank when there is nothing to divide by.

CMS database

What an editor authored or configured: titles, deities, pins, active state. Always present, never estimated.

Analytics warehouse

What devotees actually did, inside the selected date range. Absent or approximate when events are missing.

Worked out

Derived on the fly from the other two. Blank means the divisor was zero — not that the value is zero.

Status pinCMS

The position someone deliberately pinned this to in the Status feed. Blank for most items — pinning is the exception, not the rule. Only counts while the pin is live: not deleted, and inside its start/end window.

Sourcepinned_content.pin_position for the all-gods surface, falling back to the per-deity surface

Home pinCMS

Same idea, for the home feed. Also usually blank.

Sourcepinned_content on the home surface, matched through the item's home-feed card

Avg. positionWarehouse

Where this item was actually seen, averaged across every view in the window. 1.0 means top of feed. This is an outcome, not a setting — you cannot change it directly, and it moves as the catalogue rotates.

Sourceaverage position_index on status_viewed, shifted to be 1-based for reading

On home sinceCMS

When this item's home-feed card was created. Blank does not mean "not on the homepage" — older items that have never been re-edited simply have no card record.

Sourcehome_feed_items.created_at

ViewsWarehouse

How many times the item was viewed in the window. Counts views in the Status tab only — see the caveats below.

Sourcestatus_viewed events

ViewersWarehouse

Roughly how many different people saw it. Approximate by design — about half a percent out — because exact counting at this scale is far more expensive and never changes a decision.

Sourceapproximate distinct count of users on status_viewed

Share intentsWarehouse

How many times someone tapped Share — before choosing where to send it. Only recorded from 12 September 2026. Earlier ranges show blank.

Sourcestatus_share_cta_clicked events

SharesWarehouse

Shares that actually completed. Failed attempts are excluded.

Sourcestatus_share_result events with a successful result

Share rateWorked out

Shares ÷ Views. The headline number and the default sort — the closest thing here to "was this worth making". Blank when nobody saw it, which is different from nobody sharing it.

DerivationShares ÷ Views

CompletionWorked out

Of the people who tapped Share, how many finished. Counts only shares from 12 September onward, so it lines up with the intents it is divided by.

DerivationShares since 12 Sep ÷ Share intents

Five things that look like bugs but are not

Each of these has caught someone already. None of them is a fault in the report.

Expected

The two tabs do not add up

Tab 2's Viewers is not the sum of Tab 1's Viewers, and never will be. One devotee who viewed three of a deity's statuses is one viewer at the deity level and three separate rows at the item level. Adding the item rows would count that person three times.

Expected

Completion can go over 100%

Completion is "shares per recorded intent", not a true funnel. A devotee who retries a failed share fires a second result, and a share that started on the home feed need not pass through the Status tab's Share button at all. So the top and bottom of the fraction count slightly different populations, and the ratio can exceed 1.

A number well above 100% is worth a look, but it is data, not a defect.

By design

Share intents and Completion are blank before 12 September 2026

The app did not record that event until then. Blank means not recorded, not zero — a zero would say devotees never tried to share, which is a different and untrue claim.

Known gap

Home-feed views are missing, but home-feed shares are not

Views are only recorded inside the Status tab, while shares are recorded from both the Status tab and the home feed. Any item getting significant home-feed traffic therefore shows an inflated share rate, and an item can legitimately show zero views alongside real shares.

Closing this needs a change in the mobile app. Until then, treat share rate as comparable between items with similar home exposure, not across the whole catalogue.

Read carefully

Avg. position is not a setting you control

It records where devotees happened to see the item, so it reflects how far people scrolled as much as where the item sat. It also blends both arms of the live feed-ordering experiment, which means a shift in this column can be a change in how the experiment splits traffic rather than anything about the content.

Ganesha-status-1 had the most views of the three and the fewest shares — the case the default sort exists to surface. Hanuman-status-5 had fewer views but converted far better at both steps.

All dates and day-counts are IST calendar days, and both ends of a date range are included. The date filter applies to events only — it never hides items from the catalogue, which is why Created and Days live stay put when you change it.
